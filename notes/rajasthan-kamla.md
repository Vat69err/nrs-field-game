## Faithfully captured but reframed for narrative

**The identity transformation (bahu → own name):** She says this herself at length (lines 277–280). In the game I give the observation first to a woman in the lane, then let Kamla confirm it herself. Her words are accurate; the staging is invented.

**The organizer's briefing:** The community organizer is present in the real interview but does not give the researcher a prior briefing. The lines I give her (*"Pehle bilkul ghunghat mein rehti thi"*, *"Woh sab khud seekha"*, the smartphone detail) are invented but accurately summarise what Kamla herself says in the transcript.

**The hitting:** She mentions it in passing (line 20), contextualised as a money problem, in a sentence that moves on immediately. The game reproduces this tone — it is mentioned, filed, not dwelt upon. The framing (*"she has long since filed and no longer needs to carry"*) is my interpretive addition.

**The sourcing problem as a "structural problem she knows precisely":** She discusses competitive pricing (lines 35–37, 141–145) as normal shop knowledge. I added the analytical framing ("one step behind on scale"). The facts are hers; the framing is mine.

## Invented / dramatised

**The SHG meeting with Kamla's empty chair:** The SHG meeting is real (the transcript takes place during/after one) but the specific scene — empty chair, organizer pointing to the shop — is invented staging.

**"A woman in the lane" commenting on the name change:** Invented character. The observation is directly from Kamla (lines 277–280) but I put an early version of it in a bystander's mouth to create a narrative entry point before the researcher meets her.

**The researcher being challenged** (*"Toh aap humari mehnat ka adhyayan kar rahe ho / Aur paise nahi doge"*): This exchange does not happen in the transcript. Kamla is warm and engaged with the researchers throughout. This scene is modelled on the UP story's ethical challenge device. It is not Kamla's voice.

**The notebook's four-column format:** She describes tracking stock (lines 30–32) but does not specify a format. Four columns is my inference.

## Significant omissions

**The Instagram account being set up live during the interview** (lines 95–113, 146–153): The female researcher literally creates a business Instagram account for Kamla — *Maa Chamunda Cosmetics* — on her phone while they are talking. Kamla watches, they discuss the name, the organizer switches between personal and business accounts to show her. This is one of the most striking moments in the transcript: someone else is giving her a digital presence in the same conversation where she has given the researchers her time for free. This is not in the game and would make a powerful scene.

**Her elder son helping with social media editing** (lines 294–296): He helps her edit photos for Instagram. An intergenerational detail — she built the business; he helps build its digital face.

**Meesho** (lines 106–108): She orders on Meesho and talks about it comfortably. A detail about her as a digital consumer, not just producer.

**Bajaj card / household credit score** (lines 372–374): She manages all household EMIs under her name; her consistent repayment has rebuilt the household's civil score. A quiet financial inclusion story inside the main story.

**Sasur ji (father-in-law) recently passed** (lines 300–304, 438): Her father-in-law died about three months before the interview. She is the only family member who visits the in-laws; her jethani (sisters-in-law) have not visited once. She frames this as part of family duty without complaint. I folded the father (her own) into the story but this thread — the in-laws, her dependability, the absent jethani — did not make it in.

**SHG leadership role in progress** (lines 438–439): She is being considered for the role of sachiv (secretary) in the CLF as the year ends. Not included.

**The "same socks" moment** (lines 401–405): She and the female interviewer discover they are wearing identical socks at the end of the interview. A small, unguarded moment of warmth that shows the texture of the relationship the researchers had built. Not included.

**The transport logistics** (lines 254–263, 389–393): She arranges auto transport for the researchers to leave the village, making calls, explaining routes, offering to drop them herself on the scooty. Shows her practical competence and generosity beyond the interview content. Not included.

**The male interviewer is from Chandigarh** (lines 235–241): She asks him where he is from with genuine curiosity. A small exchange that shows her interest in people beyond the formal interview. Not included.
